THE PROJECT

From mission
scope to results

My main responsibility was to complete an end-to-end systems project over the course of the internship. I developed a simulated architecture for an unmanned mining mission to Mars, with the objective of locating natural resources near the planet's surface and evaluating how multiple detection strategies performed.

I organized the work into four phases: project and scope definition, system architecture development, simulation and data collection, and data consolidation. This structure gave me a clear path from an open-ended mission concept to a final comparison that I could communicate to Boeing engineers, fellow interns, and my mentor.

PROJECT DEVELOPMENT

Four phases
one mission

Each phase narrowed the problem, developed the system, and converted simulation output into a useful engineering comparison.

PHASE 01

Project and scope definition

I began by defining the mission boundary and the specific problem the system needed to address: an unmanned mission capable of locating and supporting the extraction of near-surface natural resources on Mars. The concept phase was intentionally broad, with few budget constraints and substantial freedom in how the architecture could be developed. That freedom allowed me to explore the problem at the system level before narrowing the design space.

PHASE 02

System architecture development

I researched satellites, sensors, UAVs, rovers, mining equipment, and other supporting vehicles and systems. I then defined a test region on Mars and developed three candidate architectures that used different combinations of those assets to detect resources beneath the surface. This phase connected individual technologies to the mission-level functions they needed to perform.

PHASE 03

Simulation and data collection

I built three configurations combining satellites, rovers, and UAVs. The concepts varied in equipment count, cost, search-pattern complexity, and collection frequency. I also developed a cost comparison that considered fuel, transportation, repairs, and equipment. After defining the architectures, I ran repeated simulations in Ansys STK and randomized the locations of the subsurface resources to create different search conditions for each run.

PHASE 04

Data consolidation and presentation

I consolidated the simulation results into clear charts that compared all three configurations across cost, resource-location efficiency, and feasibility. I then assembled the project into a final presentation and communicated my findings to Boeing engineers, other interns, and my mentor.
