THE RESEARCH

Building the case
from first principles

The premise of this research is to develop a CFD case that captures cavitation behavior within a converging-diverging nozzle. The immediate work focuses on building a stable geometry, mesh, and pressure-based simulation that can be checked against experimental behavior before introducing additional variables.

The longer-term goal is to understand whether cavitation can be intentionally controlled and used as part of a water-treatment process. Because the project is still in progress, the current portfolio reflects the model-development and mesh-verification stages rather than presenting a completed water-purification result.

BACKGROUND RESEARCH

From fluid mechanics
to cavitating flow

I began by returning to the core fluid-mechanics principles behind the case, then used scientific literature to identify the higher-level physics and numerical approach needed for cavitation modeling.

Pressure gradientsHow acceleration through the contraction and throat produces the pressure drop that can initiate cavitation.
Internal flowHow confined flow develops through the nozzle and responds to rapid changes in cross-sectional area.
Boundary layersHow near-wall behavior and surface conditions influence losses, separation, and the local pressure field.
Venturi geometryHow contraction, throat, and diffuser dimensions shape velocity recovery and cavitation behavior.

MESH CONVERGENCE

Choosing the model
with purpose

I compared coarse, medium, and fine meshes across inlet velocities from 0.30 to 1.15 m/s, using pressure difference as the convergence quantity and the experimental curve as the reference. The medium mesh provided the best balance between agreement and computational efficiency, so it became the working model represented by the geometry above.

Selected direction

Continue the research with the medium mesh so that future roughness cases remain computationally manageable while preserving the established pressure trend.

NEXT STEPS

From a smooth wall
to material selection

The next research phase will test the validated working model under multiple wall-roughness cases. By extracting the water and vapor volume fractions, I will compare the amount and distribution of cavitation and determine whether surface roughness can be used to increase cavitation in a controlled way for future water-purification studies.

01

Define roughness cases

Create a controlled range of wall-roughness inputs while keeping the geometry, mesh strategy, and operating conditions consistent.

02

Run comparable simulations

Use the medium mesh for each case so changes in the solution can be tied to roughness rather than a changing discretization.

03

Extract phase fractions

Measure water and vapor volume fractions to quantify where cavitation develops and how its magnitude changes with surface condition.

04

Connect to purification

Use the roughness comparison to identify promising material or surface conditions for later water-treatment testing.
